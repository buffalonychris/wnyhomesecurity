## 2. Purpose

This document governs the digital experience, visual language, interaction posture, and conversion behavior of the WNY Home Security replacement beta website.

The replacement website is a complete sales and education experience, not a cosmetic reskin, theme exercise, package-comparison site, hardware catalog, alarm-company template, or copy of production.

The governing experience direction is **warm architectural intelligence**: calm residential warmth, disciplined architectural composition, credible technology evidence, and clear human guidance. The site should feel premium and memorable without becoming cold, theatrical, confusing, inaccessible, or slow.

This document creates no production copy, route, navigation, source, CSS, asset, runtime, analytics, integration, deployment, or external-system authority.

## 5. Business and experience positioning

WNY Home Security should be understood as a locally accountable intelligent-property company that professionally designs, installs, integrates, configures, tests, explains, and supports customer-owned solutions.

The public experience must lead with:

- practical outcomes;
- ownership and meaningful control;
- privacy and local-first posture where supported;
- one coherent customer experience for supported integrations;
- professional customization;
- expandability;
- honest capabilities;
- local accountability.

Equipment may appear as credible supporting evidence, but device counts and technical components must not become the public value proposition.

## 6. Immediate-understanding standard

Within approximately five seconds, a new visitor should understand:

1. WNY Home Security professionally designs and installs intelligent-property solutions.
2. The solutions can combine security, automation, safety, lighting, aging support, and property management.
3. Supported controls are brought together into one coherent experience.
4. The customer owns the system and has a clear next step.

Within approximately ten seconds, the visitor should feel:

- oriented rather than overwhelmed;
- intrigued rather than confused;
- calm rather than frightened;
- confident that the company is professional;
- invited to explore a relevant solution.

The hero and first transition must provide this understanding without requiring a visitor to know Home Assistant, protocols, product brands, or internal package terminology.

## 7. Visitor emotional sequence

The intended sequence is:

1. **Attention:** a distinctive property scene and concise promise earn notice.
2. **Understanding:** the visitor recognizes the business and its outcomes.
3. **Personal relevance:** the six pillars and real-life situations reveal a problem or opportunity that applies to the visitor.
4. **Trust:** ownership, privacy, local accountability, process, and honest limitations reduce skepticism.
5. **Desire:** coherent dashboard and lived-outcome evidence make the improved property feel tangible.
6. **Confidence:** customization and expandability show that the visitor is not buying a rigid box.
7. **Reduced uncertainty:** education, process, support, and expectation-setting answer objections.
8. **Action:** a clear, qualified next step feels useful rather than pressured.

Visitors who are not ready to convert should receive an education path. Existing customers should reach support without being forced through a sales journey.

## 9. Visual-quality direction

The site should feel:

- polished and intentional;
- premium but approachable;
- technologically sophisticated but human;
- residentially warm and regionally credible;
- spacious but not empty;
- visually rich but easy to scan;
- consistent without becoming monotonous;
- distinctive without relying on novelty.

### 9.1 Composition

- Use disciplined content widths and deliberate focal points.
- Prefer one dominant idea per viewport-sized composition.
- Use asymmetry where it supports hierarchy, balanced by stable alignment.
- Let imagery and text share a clear relationship; do not float unrelated copy over decorative backgrounds.
- Use generous whitespace to separate decisions, not to artificially lengthen the page.
- Use cards for comparable choices or bounded evidence, not as the default container for every sentence.

### 9.2 Depth and surfaces

- Depth should come from controlled contrast, soft elevation, overlapping media planes, and restrained borders.
- Dark emphasis panels should mark important moments, not become the default background for every section.
- Gradients may shape focus or bridge themes; they must not become decorative noise.
- Icons must use one coherent, accessible family and must support comprehension.

### 9.3 Rejected visual patterns

Reject generic contractor templates, hardware-store grids, tactical surveillance imagery, cold SaaS dashboards, gaming interfaces, crypto-style neon, excessive glass effects, random gradients, excessive rounded containers, dense walls of equal-priority cards, and disconnected stock-photo montages.

## 10. Photography and generated-image authority

### 10.1 Image strategy

Primary imagery should show the customer outcome in a believable property context. Use a purposeful mix of:

- architectural exteriors and interiors;
- wide environmental scenes for context;
- mid-range scenes for human use and property behavior;
- close detail for credible equipment or interaction;
- dashboard views as evidence;
- daylight, dusk, night, and seasonal Western New York conditions where relevant.

Photography should feel calm, premium, lived-in, and regionally credible. Snow, rain, darkness, and changing weather may demonstrate relevance without creating fear.

### 10.2 Outcome-led subjects

Approved subject directions include arriving home safely, useful exterior lighting, a customer checking supported property status from one place, selected water awareness, easier access, consent-aware aging support, second-property awareness, and automations reducing daily friction.

### 10.3 Device imagery

Devices may appear:

- in installed context;
- at a scale that supports credibility;
- when their presence clarifies how an outcome is achieved;
- without turning the composition into a catalog.

Do not use floating-device walls, false product geometry, impossible installations, or vendor branding as the main experience.

### 10.4 Generated imagery

Generated imagery is permitted only as a future governed asset candidate. Review must cover:

- realism and architectural coherence;
- device and installation accuracy;
- human anatomy and behavior;
- lighting and reflection consistency;
- Western New York credibility;
- claims implications;
- legibility under text;
- desktop and mobile crops;
- dark and light modes;
- alternative-text intent;
- provenance and approval status.

Generated imagery must not be represented as an actual customer installation, testimonial, case study, or verified property.

## 11. Hero experience

### 11.1 Recommended composition

The replacement hero should use an art-directed still-image composition as the default:

- a premium Western New York residential or small-property setting at dusk;
- warm architectural light against a controlled charcoal or trust-navy emphasis field;
- one clear focal area for the property;
- one restrained dashboard/device evidence layer;
- a concise message block with strong contrast;
- primary and secondary actions;
- a compact trust strip or proof transition;
- visual continuity into HOME-002.

A split or offset composition is preferred when it preserves message legibility and allows the property and dashboard evidence to coexist. Full bleed is acceptable only when crop, contrast, and focal-point controls remain reliable across sizes.

### 11.2 Still image versus video

Still imagery is the default comprehension and performance layer. Optional short, silent motion or video may be evaluated later only when:

- the first meaningful message and CTA do not wait for it;
- a high-quality poster/still is complete;
- reduced-motion behavior is equivalent;
- captions or controls are supplied when content requires them;
- mobile bandwidth and low-power devices are protected;
- the media proves a real experience advantage.

### 11.3 Hero rejection criteria

No carousel, package-price promotion, busy collage, full-screen loading film, illegible text-over-image, technical diagram, generic family stock banner, fear imagery, or unsupported capability demonstration.

`public/brand/heros/HomePageHero.png` remains the current production asset and is not changed by this authority.

## 12. Typography authority

Manrope remains the heading/display family and Inter remains the body/UI family.

### 12.1 Hierarchy

- Display headlines should be short, direct, and visually dominant without consuming most of a phone screen.
- Section headlines should identify one idea and support scanning.
- Body copy should use comfortable measure, typically about 45–75 characters per line.
- Labels and eyebrows should be brief and subordinate.
- Buttons should use concise sentence-case action language.
- Numeric evidence should be legible and contextual, never decorative vanity data.

### 12.2 Behavior

- Use semantic type tokens and fluid responsive scaling.
- Favor sentence case over all caps.
- Avoid extremely thin weights and compressed tracking.
- Keep centered paragraphs short; use left alignment for extended explanation.
- Preserve adequate line height and paragraph spacing.
- Prevent orphaned single words in important display copy where practical.
- Maintain readable type and hierarchy under browser zoom and text resizing.

Exact sizes and loading implementation belong to the future design-system and implementation tasks.

## 13. Color and theme authority

Dark and light are complete expressions of one brand. They must preserve the same information, hierarchy, interaction, and capability.

### 13.1 Initial and override behavior

- On first visit, use the operating-system preference.
- Provide a clearly labeled theme control available from the global experience.
- A user override should persist locally and take precedence over system preference.
- Provide a way to return to system preference.
- Do not use theme preference for behavioral profiling.
- Prevent a misleading flash of the wrong theme during initialization.

Exact storage and implementation mechanism remain deferred.

### 13.2 Dark experience

Dark mode should feel cinematic, focused, premium, and technologically advanced while retaining warm human cues. Use layered charcoal/trust-navy surfaces, warm highlights, disciplined borders, and high-legibility text.

It must not become pure-black, oppressive, tactical, neon-heavy, cold, or difficult to read.

### 13.3 Light experience

Light mode should feel warm, architectural, spacious, clear, and trustworthy. Use a warm off-white canvas, clean light surfaces, controlled charcoal/trust-navy emphasis, and premium gold action accents.

It must not become sterile, washed out, generic, low-contrast, or visually unrelated to dark mode.

### 13.4 Mode parity

Both modes must preserve:

- readable hierarchy;
- accessible contrast;
- visible focus;
- image intent and believable treatment;
- dashboard status meaning;
- CTA priority;
- form/error/success meaning;
- footer and navigation clarity;
- reduced-motion support.

All colors, surfaces, borders, shadows, gradients, and states must use approved semantic tokens. No uncontrolled hardcoded colors are authorized.

## 14. Button, link, and CTA authority

### 14.1 Action hierarchy

| Action type | Role | Visual posture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary CTA | One qualified next step on the page | Highest contrast, confident, restrained |
| Secondary CTA | Explore relevant solutions or supporting path | Clear outline or lower-emphasis filled treatment |
| Tertiary action | Education or optional detail | Text or low-emphasis control |
| Existing-customer action | Support, service, or expansion | Clearly labeled and visually distinct from prospect conversion |
| Navigation link | Orientation and movement | Stable, readable, clear current state |
| Card action | Open one understood destination | Whole-card behavior only when semantics and accessibility are clear |
| Form submit | Complete an understood action | Explicit label, loading, result feedback |
| Icon button | Compact utility | Accessible name, adequate target, visible state |

### 14.2 CTA language

Preferred patterns:

- Request a Property Assessment
- Explore Solutions
- See What Your Property Can Do
- Design Your System
- Talk Through Your Goals
- Get Customer Support
- Explore Expansion Options

“Schedule a Property Assessment” may be used only when the destination actually provides governed scheduling. Until that is verified, use request-oriented language.

Do not use Buy a Package, Choose a Package, Shop Hardware, Pick a Tier, vague “Learn More” repetition, false urgency, or misleading labels.

Final deployed labels remain subject to COPYAUTH001, claims review, and destination truth.

### 14.3 States

Every relevant interactive control must have default, hover where supported, visible focus, pressed/active, disabled, loading, success, and error states.

Feedback should use restrained elevation, border/surface change, controlled illumination, and modest icon or directional movement. Do not pulse, bounce, use excessive glow, or hide meaning in motion.

## 15. Interaction-state authority

- Keyboard focus must be visible and at least as clear as hover.
- Touch devices must not depend on hover.
- Pressed state must communicate activation immediately.
- Disabled controls must remain legible and explain why when the reason is not obvious.
- Loading states must prevent duplicate action without making the interface appear frozen.
- Success and error feedback must be announced accessibly and must not rely on color alone.
- Accordions, menus, dialogs, toggles, and media controls must expose state semantically.
- Interactive cards must not contain conflicting nested actions.
- Touch targets must be practical for phone use and separated to prevent accidental activation.

## 16. Navigation-experience authority

Production navigation remains locked. UXAUTH001 defines future experience principles only.

The future replacement navigation must provide:

- simple top-level choices;
- constant brand and page orientation;
- clear current-page and current-section indication;
- access to all six pillars in canonical order;
- clear assessment and education paths;
- a persistent but non-obstructive support path;
- parent, homepage, related-content, and contextual-next-step paths;
- useful deep-link entry;
- a structured footer;
- minimal cognitive load.

At meaningful points, a visitor must be able to answer:

1. Where am I?
2. Which broader section contains this page?
3. How do I return to the parent or homepage?
4. What is the most useful next step?
5. Where do I request an assessment?
6. Where do I get support?

The browser Back button is not the site’s wayfinding system. Final labels, menus, routes, breadcrumbs, and redirects belong to NAVIA001.

## 17. Mobile-navigation authority

The recommended mobile posture is:

- a compact top header with brand identity, theme control, and accessible menu trigger;
- a clear menu panel or full-screen navigation surface with current state, six-pillar access, support, and primary action;
- contextual actions within page flow;
- a persistent assessment action only when it does not obstruct reading, controls, safe areas, or accessibility;
- no permanent multi-item app-style bottom navigation unless NAVIA001 proves that it improves tested journeys.

The menu must support keyboard and screen-reader use, predictable dismissal, focus management, body-scroll control, safe areas, orientation change, and deep-link entry. State persistence must not trap a user in an open or stale menu.

## 18. Dashboard-presentation authority

The customer-facing dashboard visual should demonstrate one coherent system without looking like a developer interface, network console, or fictional command center.

Preferred customer-facing labels include Property Dashboard, System Dashboard, Property Control, System Overview, One Dashboard, and Unified Control.

### 18.1 Information hierarchy

1. Overall property state and urgent supported conditions.
2. Common controls and scenes.
3. Security, access, camera, lighting, safety, environmental, and property groupings.
4. Rooms, areas, devices, history, and secondary detail.

Use card size, placement, grouping, iconography, and semantic status color to communicate priority. Do not give every tile equal emphasis.

### 18.2 Realism

Mockups may use realistic fictitious data only. They must not expose customer data, imply impossible universal control, or show unsupported autonomous or emergency authority.

Desktop should show useful breadth, tablet should preserve direct control, and mobile should prioritize status and frequent actions. Both themes must preserve status meaning.

## 19. Motion and transition authority

Motion exists to guide attention, clarify state, preserve continuity, and add restrained polish.

### 19.1 Semantic motion categories

Future token governance should define:

| Token category | Recommended range | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motion instant | 80–120 ms | Pressed feedback and immediate state acknowledgement |
| Motion quick | 140–200 ms | Hover, focus-adjacent surface response, compact control changes |
| Motion standard | 220–320 ms | Menus, accordions, card transitions, theme-safe UI changes |
| Motion deliberate | 360–500 ms | Restrained section reveal or major spatial transition |

Recommended easing roles are `motion-ease-standard`, `motion-ease-enter`, and `motion-ease-exit`. Exact values require the future token owner. Durations must be semantic tokens, not scattered constants.

### 19.2 Permitted use

- restrained section reveals;
- subtle image-depth changes;
- card elevation and button feedback;
- icon direction or state changes;
- dashboard state transitions;
- menu and accordion transitions;
- theme transition after usable initial paint;
- progress and success feedback.

### 19.3 Restricted use

No scroll hijacking, forced horizontal scrolling, autoplay audio, continuous decorative background motion, excessive parallax, long loading sequences, bouncing CTAs, layout-shifting animation, or motion before basic content is usable.

Reduced-motion preference must remove nonessential movement and replace spatial animation with immediate or low-motion state change.

## 20. Section-transition authority

The homepage should use contrast and pacing to create chapters:

- HOME-001 may be the strongest dark/cinematic moment.
- HOME-002 should resolve into clear, lighter explanation.
- HOME-003 should provide structured discoverability.
- HOME-004 and HOME-005 should use calm proof and process rhythm.
- HOME-006 may reintroduce immersive imagery.
- HOME-007 should use visual continuity or progressive expansion.
- HOME-008 should become quieter and editorial.
- HOME-009 should be unmistakably service-oriented.
- HOME-010 may return to a controlled premium emphasis before the footer.

Transitions may use whitespace, media scale, surface change, composition, and restrained motion. Avoid arbitrary diagonal shapes, random gradients, repetitive zig-zag layouts, abrupt theme changes, or padding without narrative purpose.

## 21. Responsive-behavior authority

Responsive design is intentional re-composition, not desktop shrinkage.

### 21.1 Required ranges

Future implementation must explicitly test wide desktop, standard desktop, laptop, tablet landscape, tablet portrait, large phone, and small phone.

### 21.2 Adaptation rules

- Preserve message and action priority across widths.
- Recompose the hero rather than merely cropping it.
- Use art-directed crops and focal-point metadata.
- Stack CTAs without changing their hierarchy.
- Reflow cards into meaningful groups and canonical order.
- Convert navigation through NAVIA001-approved behavior.
- Prioritize dashboard status and common actions on smaller screens.
- Reduce spacing and motion proportionally without creating crowding.
- Preserve readable line length and practical touch targets.
- Keep existing-customer support visible.
- Avoid horizontal overflow and clipped focus rings.
- Reevaluate media weight and autoplay posture for mobile networks.

The primary message and CTA must be clear on a phone without excessive preliminary scrolling.

## 22. Conversion authority

### 22.1 Conversion model

The replacement site should move qualified visitors through:

Understanding → Relevance → Trust → Differentiation → Evidence → Reduced Risk → Clear Next Step

The preferred primary action is a truthful request for a property assessment, estimate, or goals conversation. Exact terminology and destination must be verified against current funnel and scheduling authority.

### 22.2 Action roles

- **Primary:** request a qualified next step.
- **Secondary:** explore solutions or the six pillars.
- **Education:** answer a concern without forcing contact.
- **Existing customer:** support, service, or expansion.

Repeat the primary CTA after the visitor has received enough context, not after every paragraph. Set expectations before form entry and provide reassuring, accurate confirmation afterward.

### 22.3 Friction discipline

- Ask only for information needed at the current step.
- Explain why information is requested when not obvious.
- Preserve entered data when safe and authorized.
- Use specific labels, helpful errors, and clear recovery.
- Do not use false urgency, fake scarcity, countdowns, hidden fees, preselected consent, or dark patterns.

This authority does not change any form or funnel behavior.

- clear local service context;
- realistic property and dashboard evidence;
- a transparent professional process;
- clear next steps and support paths;
- qualified capability language;
- honest dependencies and limits;
- authorized installation photography;
- authorized testimonials or case studies;
- warranties, credentials, partnerships, or statistics only when documented.

Do not invent reviews, counts, years, awards, certifications, partner status, guarantees, customer work, or performance data.

## 24. Content-experience authority

Final copy belongs to COPYAUTH001 and the claims owner. Experience content should be:

- plain language;
- concise and scannable;
- outcome-first;
- credible and useful;
- technically accurate;
- specific enough to distinguish the company;
- free of unnecessary jargon.

Headlines should communicate one benefit or decision. Subheads should add meaning, not repeat the headline. Cards should be independently understandable. Technical detail should appear after the visitor understands why it matters. Forms, errors, and confirmations should state what happened and what comes next.

- meaningful links and control names;

- non-color-only meaning;
- practical touch targets;
- labeled fields and useful errors;
- screen-reader status announcements;
- alternative text based on image purpose;
- decorative-image suppression;
- reduced-motion support;

## 26. Performance authority

Premium quality must be perceived through clarity, composition, and responsiveness—not excessive payload.

Future experience-level requirements:

- useful hero text and CTA render before optional media enhancement;
- responsive images use appropriate sizes, formats, crops, and lazy-loading posture;
- fonts do not block understanding or cause unstable layout;
- video is exceptional, optimized, controlled, and replaceable by an equivalent poster experience;
- motion avoids layout shift and main-thread obstruction;
- theme initialization avoids wrong-theme flash;
- interactions become available quickly on mobile networks and modest devices;
- below-the-fold media does not compete with initial content.

Do not authorize oversized video, unnecessary animation libraries, excessive image weight, blocking effects, or polish that degrades usability. Exact budgets and implementation architecture remain deferred.

## 28. Experience rejection criteria

Reject a replacement experience that:

- leads with packages, tiers, hardware, or price;
- resembles a generic alarm dealer, contractor, store, or SaaS template;
- uses fear, coercion, false urgency, or unsupported claims;
- looks impressive but is hard to understand;
- hides support or confuses prospects and customers;
- uses noncanonical pillar labels or order;
- centers Home Assistant on the homepage;
- uses a dark neon default or weak light mode;
- breaks mode parity, tokens, accessibility, or reduced motion;
- uses meaningless stock media or false installation proof;
- shows impossible dashboards or universal compatibility;
- implies professional monitoring, emergency dispatch, guaranteed prevention, or universal offline operation;
- makes navigation depend on the browser Back button;
- performs poorly on mobile;
- sacrifices conversion clarity for novelty;
- modifies protected systems without separate authority.
