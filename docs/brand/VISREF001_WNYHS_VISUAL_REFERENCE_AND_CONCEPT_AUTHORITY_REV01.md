## 5. Governing visual idea

The selected direction is **Warm Architectural Intelligence**.

The replacement site should feel like a beautifully designed Western New York property whose technology is quietly making life easier. Architecture and lived outcomes lead. Technology appears as credible evidence—not as a wall of devices or fictional command center.

The intended impression is:

> Calm, locally accountable property intelligence—designed around real life.

The experience should combine residential warmth, architectural discipline, meaningful depth, and believable system evidence. It must not depend on novelty effects, fear, technical spectacle, or visual excess.

## 6. Concept comparison

### 6.1 Concept A — Warm Architectural Intelligence

- **Emotional character:** Calm, capable, human, premium, trustworthy, quietly exciting.
- **Architectural treatment:** Real residential and small-business spaces; disciplined grids; purposeful whitespace; layered rooms, thresholds, windows, and approaches.
- **Technology treatment:** Dashboard and device evidence is integrated into the property story.
- **Light and dark:** Warm light mode for explanation and trust; charcoal/trust-navy dark moments for emphasis and premium depth.
- **Imagery:** Regionally credible WNY properties, realistic seasons, ordinary life improved by coordinated systems.
- **Materials:** Warm painted surfaces, wood, stone, glass, metal, soft textile detail, controlled reflections.
- **Typography:** Manrope architectural headings; Inter body and interface text.
- **Cards:** Selective grouping with varied scale and clear hierarchy; no uniform card wall.
- **Buttons:** Direct, stable, tactile-looking, clearly prioritized, restrained illumination.
- **Dashboard:** Believable customer proof with status and common actions above technical detail.
- **Transitions:** Surface, light, media scale, and spacing create narrative movement.
- **Motion:** Semantic, short, optional, and comprehension-safe.
- **Mobile:** Property focal point, message, and primary action survive; evidence becomes stacked and selective.
- **Strengths:** Best balance of brand fit, conversion clarity, warmth, credibility, themes, and regional relevance.
- **Risks:** Can become generic luxury-home design if accountability and system evidence are too subtle.
- **Conversion fit:** Strong.
- **Accessibility fit:** Strong.
- **Performance fit:** Strong with art-directed stills and selective media.
- **Brand fit:** Highest.

### 6.2 Concept B — Cinematic Property Control

- **Emotional character:** Dramatic, advanced, immersive, commanding.
- **Architectural treatment:** Full-bleed dusk/night properties, deep shadows, illuminated zones, stronger asymmetry.
- **Technology treatment:** Larger dashboard, camera, access, and status layers.
- **Light and dark:** Dark-led presentation with light sections as relief.
- **Imagery:** Twilight exteriors, weather, illuminated paths, property-awareness cues.
- **Materials:** Dark glass, charcoal metal, controlled glow, reflective stone, crisp screens.
- **Typography:** Large display moments with compressed supporting copy.
- **Cards:** Dark floating evidence planes and spatial overlays.
- **Buttons:** Stronger contrast and illuminated focus.
- **Dashboard:** Cinematic proof surface spanning multiple property domains.
- **Transitions:** Media-led reveals and controlled depth.
- **Motion:** Environmental motion and state reveals could support atmosphere.
- **Mobile:** Requires aggressive simplification to avoid a poster or game interface.
- **Strengths:** Memorable hero and strong premium impression.
- **Risks:** Can imply surveillance, luxury exclusivity, impossible capability, cold SaaS, gaming, or performance-heavy spectacle.
- **Conversion fit:** Medium.
- **Accessibility fit:** Medium.
- **Performance fit:** Medium-to-low if effects become structural.
- **Brand fit:** Useful as limited emphasis, not the governing direction.

### 6.3 Concept C — Calm Precision / Invisible Infrastructure

- **Emotional character:** Clear, intelligent, orderly, reassuring, editorial.
- **Architectural treatment:** Light-led grids, large margins, property details, restrained evidence.
- **Technology treatment:** Mostly invisible until status, control, or outcome needs explanation.
- **Light and dark:** Light leads; dark is a focused alternate expression.
- **Imagery:** Daylight interiors, exterior details, natural human use, useful outcomes.
- **Materials:** Warm paper, plaster, pale wood, brushed metal, minimal glass.
- **Typography:** Editorial hierarchy, shorter lines, clear annotations.
- **Cards:** Few, precise, low-depth surfaces with strong grouping.
- **Buttons:** Quiet but unmistakable.
- **Dashboard:** Compact, understandable, evidence-driven.
- **Transitions:** Whitespace and typography carry narrative change.
- **Motion:** Minimal state confirmation.
- **Mobile:** Naturally adaptable and highly readable.
- **Strengths:** Excellent clarity, accessibility, performance, and comprehension.
- **Risks:** Can feel generic, understate system breadth, or lack emotional pull.
- **Conversion fit:** Strong for understanding; medium for desire.
- **Accessibility fit:** Highest.
- **Performance fit:** Highest.
- **Brand fit:** Strong supporting language, not distinctive enough alone.

## 7. Selected direction and permitted borrowing

**Concept A — Warm Architectural Intelligence is selected as the primary visual direction.**

It best supports the approved emotional sequence from attention through confident action while making a complicated offering understandable, useful, and credible. It places real life ahead of technology without hiding the coordinated intelligence that differentiates WNY Home Security.

Future concepts may borrow:

- from Concept B: one controlled cinematic hero or media moment, deeper focus surfaces, and twilight atmosphere;
- from Concept C: editorial clarity, disciplined whitespace, concise annotation, and mobile-first restraint.

They may not import Concept B's dark-default spectacle, surveillance mood, or effect-heavy motion, or Concept C's generic minimalism and under-expression of system breadth.

## 8. Visual-language authority

### 8.1 Composition

- Use a disciplined grid with intentional breaks for property media and system evidence.
- Favor offset or asymmetric balance when it clarifies hierarchy.
- Use spacing to separate narrative jobs, not to imitate premium quality through emptiness.
- Alternate wide architectural moments with tighter explanatory sections.
- Maintain readable line lengths and stable alignment anchors.
- Connect property, outcome, and evidence through foreground/background relationships.

### 8.2 Density and rhythm

- One dominant idea per viewport region.
- Use varied density: immersive hero, clear promise, structured pillars, proof, process, outcomes, expansion, education, and support.
- Avoid identical card grids section after section.
- Allow dense dashboard evidence only when hierarchy remains immediate.
- Mobile reduces supporting detail before meaning.

### 8.3 Depth and surfaces

- Depth should explain importance, containment, focus, or state.
- Prefer layered opaque surfaces, disciplined borders, controlled shadows, and selective translucency.
- Blur and transparency may support credible interface overlays but may not impair contrast or become the brand.
- Avoid excessive glass, heavy shadows, floating-everything composition, and decorative depth.

### 8.4 Geometry

- Use stable architectural rectangles, clean alignment, and selective softened corners.
- Radii must come from semantic tokens and reflect component role.
- Avoid playful bubbles, excessive pills, arbitrary diagonals, and juvenile or experimental shapes.

### 8.5 Light, color, and material

- Light guides attention to entrances, lived zones, key outcomes, and actions.
- Warm illumination provides humanity; charcoal/trust-navy provides focus; approved gold accents indicate premium action or detail.
- Environmental texture may include rain on glass, snow light, wood, stone, painted walls, and evening atmosphere.
- All colors, gradients, shadows, borders, radii, transparency, blur, highlights, and motion require approved semantic tokens. VISREF001 authorizes no raw value.

## 9. Photography authority

### 9.1 Subject priorities

Show:

- recognizable WNY residential and small-business properties;
- entrances, living spaces, halls, kitchens, storefronts, offices, paths, garages, and secondary zones;
- daylight, twilight, night, snow, rain, and seasonal relevance without fear;
- people, pets, vehicles, and outdoor systems only where they support a real outcome;
- cameras, locks, lighting, sensors, controls, and robotic property care as installed parts of a coherent environment;
- customer control and awareness without surveillance theater.

### 9.2 Camera and lens character

- Use natural architectural perspective with minimal distortion.
- Camera height should feel human and present, not aerial, floor-level, or security-camera-like.
- Use environmental frames for context and detail frames for proof.
- Focal depth guides attention while keeping installations and relationships understandable.
- Avoid extreme wide-angle distortion, synthetic tilt-shift, purposeless drone views, and shallow focus that hides the outcome.

### 9.3 Light and finish

- Favor plausible practical light, windows, warm fixtures, and controlled exterior illumination.
- Preserve natural materials and skin tones.
- Use restrained grain, realistic sharpness, moderate contrast, and recoverable shadow detail.
- Avoid hyper-HDR, crushed blacks, orange-and-teal grading, plastic surfaces, excessive bloom, and impossible light.

### 9.4 Framing and placement

- Reserve negative space for headlines and actions without obvious staging.
- Protect the subject at desktop, tablet, and mobile crops.
- Place evidence near the property or story it explains.
- Text overlays require reliable contrast and may not cover faces, critical devices, entrances, or safety information.

## 10. Generated-image authority

Generated imagery may support future concept work only after evaluation for:

- architectural realism and WNY credibility;
- correct property, device, installation, and environmental relationships;
- plausible lighting, reflections, shadows, screens, and materials;
- anatomically credible people, hands, pets, and interactions;
- continuity across adjacent images or sequence frames;
- physically plausible cameras, locks, lighting, sensors, wiring, and mounting;
- no unsupported dashboard behavior, monitoring claim, emergency response, or impossible integration;
- reliable desktop/mobile crops and dark/light treatment;
- accessibility, contrast, cognitive load, and text-free interpretation;
- brand fit and absence of stock-like or science-fiction styling.

Every generated image receives exactly one status:

- **APPROVED PRODUCTION** — requires a later asset-owner task and manual approval; unavailable under VISREF001.
- **MOCKUP-ONLY** — may support design review but cannot ship.
- **COMPOSITION REFERENCE** — only framing, light, or layout qualities may be reused.
- **REJECTED** — may not be used.
- **REGENERATE** — direction may continue after named defects are corrected.

No generated image becomes production authority here.

## 13. Hero concept authority

### 13.1 Selected posture

- **Property-first with human evidence:** the property is stable; a person may provide scale or context without generic family-stock treatment.
- **Dashboard as proof:** one believable phone or larger dashboard layer may show coherent awareness and control.
- **Contained versus full bleed:** offset or split is preferred. Full bleed requires reliable focus, contrast, crop, loading, and readability.
- **Time:** twilight is preferred for premium emphasis; daylight supports page balance and light theme.
- **Still versus motion:** an art-directed still is default. Later motion requires its own task, poster fallback, performance budget, reduced-motion equivalence, and manual approval.

### 13.2 Message relationship

The headline and primary action remain fastest to understand. Media supports the promise and cannot force unsafe contrast or subordinate the CTA to spectacle.

### 13.3 Mobile and loading

The hero requires an art-directed mobile crop, protected focus, readable copy region, visible primary action, stable dimensions, and useful fallback before enhancement.

### 13.4 Current production asset

`public/brand/heros/HomePageHero.png` remains production. HOMEHERO001 owns any final replacement. VISREF001 does not alter or approve it.

## 14. Dark and light visual direction

Dark and light are equal expressions, not inversion.

### 14.1 Dark

Dark should feel focused, cinematic, calm, and premium. Use layered charcoal/trust-navy surfaces, warm property light, disciplined boundaries, and legible text. Avoid pure-black emptiness, neon, tactical surveillance, excessive glow, and invisible borders.

### 14.2 Light

Light should feel warm, architectural, spacious, and trustworthy. Use warm off-white canvases, readable light surfaces, controlled dark emphasis, and selective premium accents. Avoid sterile SaaS white, weak contrast, and unrelated language.

### 14.3 Parity

Both modes preserve information, action hierarchy, state meaning, dashboard credibility, image intent, contrast, focus, disabled states, and reduced-motion behavior.

## 15. Buttons and interaction appearance

Behavior remains subordinate to UXAUTH001 and implementation owners.

- Primary actions look stable, confident, and unmistakable.
- Secondary actions remain visible without equal weight.
- Utility actions are quieter but never hidden.
- Hover, focus, active, loading, success, error, and disabled states remain distinct.
- Use surface, border, elevation, modest illumination, and small directional movement.
- Reject bouncing, pulsing, excessive glow, ambiguous card actions, hidden focus, and motion-dependent meaning.

## 16. Navigation visual concept

NAVIA001 controls IA, labels, destinations, route compatibility, and responsive behavior.

Navigation appearance should feel calm, spacious, clearly organized, and locally grounded. Controlled transparency over the hero is permitted only with reliable contrast/state; it should settle into a clear surface during scroll or light content. Mobile menu appearance preserves hierarchy and trust without imitating a native app shell.

No route, label, menu group, or structure is decided here.

## 17. Mobile concept

- Preserve message order and action priority.
- Use one focal image and one primary proof layer rather than a shrunken desktop collage.
- Stack pillars in canonical order and keep support visible.
- Reduce decorative depth, media weight, and simultaneous cards.
- Use comfortable touch spacing and readable type without overflow.
- Do not rely on hover, fine pointer, autoplay, or subtle color alone.
- Keep theme control understandable and persistent.
- Prefer fast still imagery and progressive enhancement on constrained networks.

## 18. Dashboard concept

- Lead with property state, priority alerts, and frequent actions.
- Group supported security, access, cameras, lighting, safety/environment, rooms, areas, scenes, and modes.
- Use scale, placement, grouping, icons, and semantic state for priority.
- Avoid equal tile walls, developer-console language, fictional metrics, universal compatibility, and impossible controls.
- Desktop may show breadth; tablet preserves control; mobile prioritizes status and common actions.
- Both themes preserve state meaning.
- Mockups remain illustrative until a dedicated owner approves representation.

## 19. Motion and transition appearance

Motion remains subordinate to UXAUTH001 semantic categories and future token authority:

- **Instant:** acknowledgement and direct state confirmation.
- **Quick:** hover, focus, menu, and control feedback.
- **Standard:** card, accordion, theme, and local section transitions.
- **Deliberate:** rare narrative media or section emphasis.

Motion clarifies origin, destination, hierarchy, and state. Reject scroll hijacking, forced horizontal movement, autoplay audio, continuous decorative backgrounds, excessive parallax, layout shift, long loading, and motion before usable content. Reduced motion provides equivalent immediate or low-motion state.

## 20. Conversion and trust visualization

Visual design must answer:

1. What does WNY Home Security do?
2. How could it help this property or person?
3. Why is it credible and different?
4. What happens next?

Trust comes from real properties, plausible installations, coherent system evidence, plain hierarchy, local accountability, qualified language, predictable actions, and no hidden pressure. Reject fake reviews, invented metrics, fabricated badges, fear, false urgency, scarcity, countdowns, and unsupported monitoring/dispatch implications.

## 21. Rejection criteria

Reject a concept that:

- conflicts with HOMEAUTH001 order, IDs, or six-pillar order;
- makes packages, hardware, subscriptions, or monitoring the public model;
- resembles a contractor template, alarm clone, cold SaaS dashboard, gaming interface, crypto product, or luxury-only showcase;
- uses surveillance fear, emergency-response implication, or unsupported capability;
- hides comprehension beneath media or motion;
- makes the whole site dark-default or weakens light mode;
- depends on uncontrolled colors, gradients, shadows, radii, or motion;
- uses inconsistent photography, impossible installations, or synthetic people/property;
- creates uniform card/effect walls;
- weakens keyboard, focus, contrast, reduced-motion, semantic, or mobile access;
- sacrifices performance for spectacle;
- changes navigation, copy, claims, routes, funnels, protected systems, or assets without their owner.
