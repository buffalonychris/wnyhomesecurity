## 1. Purpose

This document selects and governs the visual direction for the future replacement-site homepage hero. It converts the locked homepage, experience, visual, navigation, and copy authorities into a reviewable visual package without replacing the live hero or modifying production.

The hero must make the company feel like a calm, capable designer of whole-property outcomes—not a hardware reseller, alarm-company clone, dashboard vendor, or science-fiction technology brand.

## 5. Hero objectives

The future hero should:

1. establish the property as the primary subject;
2. communicate an integrated designed system without a hardware collage;
3. create immediate emotional safety and practical desirability;
4. leave reliable space for the locked headline, support, and CTAs;
5. make the Property Dashboard a supporting proof element;
6. work in desktop and mobile compositions;
7. support light and dark experiences without becoming two unrelated brands;
8. avoid unsupported installed-system, monitoring, emergency, or outcome claims;
9. remain feasible as a performant production asset after approval and optimization; and
10. invite solution discovery consistent with the Pillar-First model.

## 6. Comparative concept set

### Concept A — Architectural Property Intelligence

**Core idea:** The property is the hero. Warm architectural lighting and a small, restrained Property Dashboard proof element suggest the system quietly coordinating the whole property.

**Composition:** A believable Western New York home occupies the right side. The left side is calm negative space for copy. The dashboard remains low, small, and secondary.

**Strengths:**

- best expression of Warm Architectural Intelligence;
- strongest whole-property and solution-first posture;
- highest copy readability;
- strongest desktop-to-mobile adaptability;
- avoids making hardware or software the company’s identity; and
- naturally supports dark and light theme variants.

**Risks:** Generated review imagery must never be represented as an actual customer installation. Dashboard tiles must remain illustrative until supported by verified product behavior.

### Concept B — Property + Dashboard Split

**Core idea:** A visible customer interface shares the frame with a real property.

**Composition:** A large dashboard plane occupies the left third; a home and detached work area fill the right.

**Strengths:**

- immediately communicates unified control;
- creates a strong explanation bridge to the Property Dashboard; and
- makes system breadth visually explicit.

**Risks:**

- the interface competes with the headline;
- the company can read as a software vendor;
- the dashboard can date quickly or imply unverified functionality; and
- mobile cropping is less resilient.

### Concept C — Human-Centered Control

**Core idea:** A relaxed homeowner uses a simple property interface in a warm entry environment.

**Composition:** Human subject and phone sit on the right; the left remains text-safe.

**Strengths:**

- strongest emotional accessibility and everyday-life signal;
- makes “life made easier” tangible; and
- supports trust and local accountability.

**Risks:**

- narrows the audience and property context;
- phone UI becomes visually small;
- lifestyle casting can reduce universal relevance; and
- a single moment communicates fewer pillars than the property-first approach.

## 8. Selected direction

**Selected:** Concept A — Architectural Property Intelligence.

It is the only direction that simultaneously keeps the property, the customer outcome, the copy, and the system concept in the correct hierarchy. It supports the six-pillar promise without depicting a literal hardware inventory. It also gives future implementation the greatest flexibility to optimize, crop, art-direct, or replace the review imagery without changing the governing concept.

Concepts B and C remain comparison evidence only. They are not fallback production approvals.

## 9. Selected desktop composition

- **Aspect posture:** 16:9 source with a stable wide crop.
- **Property placement:** right 58 percent, with the main entrance and roofline preserved.
- **Copy-safe area:** left 42 percent, with generous edge and header clearance.
- **Dashboard:** lower-right, small and subordinate; five icon-only status categories.
- **Lighting:** blue-hour exterior with warm architectural light.
- **Depth:** editorial architectural photography, natural lens behavior, no artificial technology beams.
- **Visual hierarchy:** copy first, property second, dashboard proof third.

The dashboard shown in review imagery is illustrative. It does not establish production UI, actual device status, customer configuration, or a public capability claim.

## 10. Mobile composition

Mobile must be independently art-directed rather than mechanically center-cropped.

- Reserve the upper 35–42 percent as a calm text-safe zone.
- Keep the entrance and recognizable property form in the lower field.
- Stack CTAs under copy; do not place copy over detailed architecture.
- If a status strip is retained, it must remain below the primary property subject and must not compete with CTAs.
- Decorative dashboard proof may be removed at narrow widths if readability, performance, or focus suffers.
- Do not crop the home into an abstract roof or door fragment.

## 11. Light and dark theme treatment

Both themes are approved as a visual direction, not as final production assets.

### Dark

- blue-hour environment;
- white foreground copy;
- navy/charcoal atmospheric negative space;
- warm amber property light;
- muted teal status accents; and
- restrained glass treatment.

### Light

- early-morning or late-afternoon environment;
- dark navy foreground copy;
- pale warm sky and foliage as negative space;
- natural green and warm-white accents; and
- a light, low-contrast status panel.

Theme switching must not rely on CSS filters applied to one raster source. Each theme requires separately approved art direction or a production composition proven to meet contrast and crop requirements.

## 12. Text-safe overlay authority

The review overlay establishes the intended copy box:

- minimum 6 percent outer edge clearance;
- copy region must remain within the left 42 percent on wide desktop;
- headline target: no more than three visual lines at the approved desktop measure;
- support target: two to four visual lines;
- CTAs sit below support and remain outside complex image detail;
- imagery must provide sufficient native tonal stability before any supplemental gradient; and
- any production gradient must be tokenized, subtle, and independently contrast-tested.

Text must never be baked into the final hero image.

## 13. Responsive crop authority

The crop study governs intent:

- **Desktop 16:9:** text left, property right, entrance visible.
- **Tablet 3:2:** preserve a top-left copy field and the entrance; reduce or remove dashboard proof if needed.
- **Mobile 4:5:** text-safe sky/atmosphere above, property below, entrance recognizable.

Production crops require manual visual QA at minimum widths of 360, 390, 768, 1024, 1280, 1440, and 1920 pixels. CSS focal-point behavior must not be guessed from the review board.

## 14. Property-setting rules

Approved settings:

- recognizable Western New York residential or small-business property types;
- attainable, well-maintained architecture;
- seasonal context that is locally credible;
- visible entry, approach, and useful exterior lighting;
- mixed home/business cues only when the composition remains clear; and
- inclusive property types in future rotation.

Reject:

- luxury-estate fantasy;
- tropical, desert, or visibly non-local landscape;
- gated-compound or surveillance-state framing;
- police, weapons, masks, forced entry, panic, or emergency spectacle;
- stacks of devices floating around a house;
- glowing network lines or science-fiction overlays; and
- imagery presented as a verified customer installation without evidence.

## 15. Property Dashboard treatment

The dashboard may appear only as secondary proof of a unified experience.

- Use plain icon-based status categories.
- Avoid readable generated copy, invented metrics, emergency status, or monitoring claims.
- Do not imply every property receives an identical dashboard.
- Do not define implementation, card schemas, entities, devices, or automations here.
- A future implementation task must use the controlling dashboard and runtime owners.
- Customer-visible screenshots require verified data, privacy review, and separate approval.

## 16. Image-generation and asset-development specification

Future production candidates should use this direction:

**Subject:** believable Western New York property, warm architectural lighting, quiet evidence of thoughtful integration.

**Composition:** property right; clean text-safe field left on desktop; independently art-directed mobile frame; status interface small and secondary.

**Tone:** calm, capable, local, practical, modern, trustworthy, premium but attainable.

**Negative constraints:** no text, logo, watermark, tactical imagery, fear, emergency, unsupported monitoring cues, sci-fi effects, floating hardware, exaggerated luxury, or fake customer proof.

**Required review outputs:**

1. full-resolution source;
2. desktop dark candidate;
3. mobile candidate;
4. light-theme candidate or documented reason it is unnecessary;
5. text-safe overlay;
6. responsive crop study;
7. prompt/provenance record;
8. alt-text proposal;
9. dimensions, byte size, and hash; and
10. operator approval status.

## 17. Optional motion

Motion is not required and is not authorized by this task.

If later approved, acceptable motion is limited to subtle atmospheric or interface-state transitions that do not imply unsupported live behavior. No parallax that causes motion sickness, auto-playing video with essential information, flashing status, or constant dashboard animation. `prefers-reduced-motion` must receive a fully equivalent static state.

## 18. Accessibility requirements

Before production approval:

- measure foreground contrast against the actual image at all responsive crops;
- validate both themes separately;
- keep essential meaning in HTML, not the raster;
- provide concise alt text or mark the asset decorative when surrounding text fully carries meaning;
- preserve focus visibility and CTA readability;
- avoid status meaning conveyed only by color;
- ensure mobile copy is not positioned over detailed or bright image areas; and
- re-test contrast after every asset optimization or crop change.

Review images do not constitute a WCAG pass.

## 19. Performance requirements

The current PNG review assets are deliberately high-fidelity review evidence, not delivery formats.

A future production task must:

- define art-directed desktop/mobile sources;
- export responsive AVIF/WebP with an appropriate fallback;
- set intrinsic dimensions and reserve layout space;
- establish a measured visual-quality budget;
- avoid loading both theme sources when only one is displayed;
- keep dashboard proof composited efficiently or implemented separately only when authorized;
- test Largest Contentful Paint on representative mobile hardware and connection profiles; and
- never replace a production asset solely because a review image looks appealing.

## 20. Rejection criteria

Reject a candidate when any of the following is true:

- copy cannot remain readable across required crops;
- the property stops being the primary subject;
- hardware or dashboard UI becomes the hero;
- the image implies monitoring, emergency response, guaranteed prevention, or an installed customer result;
- the property feels geographically implausible or financially exclusionary;
- generated defects are visible in architecture, hardware, people, or interface;
- mobile becomes a random crop;
- light and dark variants feel like different brands;
- asset weight or rendering strategy is not realistically optimizable;
- production text is baked into the raster; or
- the asset is moved into a public path without a separately authorized implementation task.
